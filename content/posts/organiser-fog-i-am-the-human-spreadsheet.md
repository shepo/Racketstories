It's 9.40pm on a Wednesday. I'm making dinner. I'm also, in my head, counting to fourteen.

Fourteen is how many people have said they're coming to Thursday social. Well, twelve said yes, one said "probably", and Keith said "👍", which after five years I know means "yes unless it rains, or unless his knee goes, or unless there's a good documentary on". So it's thirteen and a Keith.

I've decided there's a name for this feeling. I'm calling it **organiser fog**.

![Cartoon of a flustered tennis competition organiser at a desk](organiser-desk "Me, every Wednesday, roughly. The hat is artistic licence.")

<!--more-->

## What organiser fog actually is

It isn't stress. Stress comes in spikes, like when the floodlights fail or someone turns up with their nephew who "plays a bit" (county junior, obviously). Organiser fog is quieter than that. It *hums*. It's the constant low-level sense that something needs tracking, someone needs replying to, and something written on the back of an envelope absolutely must not be lost.

You don't see it on people. Nobody at the club ever says "Bev looks fogged". What you see, eighteen months later, is the organiser quietly stepping back, and the group quietly dissolving.

I've watched it happen at our club twice. Lovely Wednesday ladies' doubles, gone. The Sunday padel lot, gone, the minute Martin moved to Bristol. Not because anyone stopped enjoying the tennis. Because the person carrying the organising put it down and nobody picked it up. Honestly, I reckon clubs lose more players to organiser fog than to bad pairings and court availability put together.

## Who I'm actually organising for

Most of the people in my WhatsApp group are what I've started calling the **forgotten middle**. They're not beginners being taken through a coaching programme. They're not on a team with fixtures. They don't have a coach booking things for them. They're just decent social players who want a good game, with the right people, ideally every week, ideally without having to think about it.

Nobody builds anything for them. And nobody builds anything for *me* either, the person organising them. Our club has software for booking courts, paying subs and running the bar tab. It has nothing whatsoever for getting fourteen people (and a Keith) onto three courts in a way that feels fair.

## The list of jobs I would like to resign from

I wrote this on the back of the scoresheet last week, which tells you everything:

- Drafting court pairings on paper
- Being the official database of who played with whom
- Reminding people to pay their £4 for balls
- Sorting WhatsApp replies on a Sunday morning
- Being the leaderboard

None of those need a *person*. They need a system. I'm a person. I would like to play tennis.

## What I'd want instead

If I could wave a racket and fix it, I'd want three things.

**Anyone can see what's on without signing up to anything.** My group includes people who still print emails. If the first step is "create an account", I've lost half of them.

**One place everyone can look.** Pairings, scores, who's on reserve. The group chat should share a *link*, not forty messages of the actual information.

**The boring bits done quietly.** Reserves moving up when someone drops out. Balancing the courts. Noticing that Sue and Raj have played together three weeks running. I want players to feel the club's well run, and I want to feel like I'm mostly just turning up.

## Why I'm bothering to write this

Because groups don't fall apart when the tennis stops being fun. They fall apart when the *organising* stops being sustainable. Whoever's carrying it gets tired and walks away, and the next person looks at the job and very sensibly says "no thank you".

If you can take enough of the fog away that *anyone* in the group could be the organiser, the group keeps going. The rally keeps going, as Jake would say. (Jake is a member at our club who builds apps, and they've been nodding along very enthusiastically while I've been moaning about all this. More on that another time.)

Right. Keith's just replied "maybe 🤔". Back to counting.

P.S. If you're an organiser and you recognise yourself in this, you're not bad at it. It's just a lot. Have a biscuit.
