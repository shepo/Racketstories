Three years ago I voted against the padel courts. I'd like that noted, because I'm about to say I was wrong, and I don't say it often. The last time was about the tea urn, and I was wrong about that too.

![Padel doubles](just-friendly "Two of these people joined last spring. One of them now does our accounts. The accounts have never been better.")

<!--more-->

My objection at the time was sensible. We're a tennis club. It says so on the sign, on the letterhead and on the mug in the committee room that nobody is allowed to use. Padel was a fad, I said. It would cost a fortune, it would be empty by the second winter, and we'd be left with two very expensive glass boxes for storing leaves.

It is now the second winter. The glass boxes are full most evenings, and the people in them are people we never had before.

That's the bit I didn't see coming. I assumed padel would take tennis players and give them somewhere else to go. It hasn't, much. It's brought in people who would never have joined a tennis club. People who tried tennis at school, were told to stand behind the baseline and keep it in, and quietly gave up. People in their thirties and forties who want a game after work and a drink after that, and who don't want to spend two years learning a second serve first.

You can hand someone a padel bat on a Tuesday and by Thursday they're having a rally. A proper one, with walls. They come off the court laughing. I've watched a man in his fifties who'd never held a racket of any kind play four games in a row and then ask the bar what the membership was. The bar didn't know. The bar now has a leaflet.

Our membership has gone up for the first time since I became chair. I've checked it three times, because I didn't believe it the first two. The bar takings are up. The average age of a member has gone down by a number I'm not allowed to publish, because the over-sixties section would take it personally.

And some of them have started playing tennis. Not many. But some. They wander past court four on the way to the car park, watch the Wednesday doubles for a bit, and ask how you get into that. That's a sentence I never heard once in the years before padel.

I still think it's a strange game. The scoring is tennis scoring, which they've stolen without asking. The walls feel like cheating to me, even though I'm assured they aren't. And it's loud. I'll grant anyone the loud.

But a club that only does what it's always done ends up with the same forty people, getting older together, wondering why nobody young ever joins. We had that club. I chaired it. I was very good at it.

So yes. I was wrong about padel. It's in the minutes now, under Any Other Business, which is where I put things I'd rather people didn't read.
