Our floodlights go off at half past nine. Not about half past nine. Half past nine. There's a timer in a grey box by the pavilion door, and the timer doesn't care what the score is.

![Club noticeboard](noticeboard "The notice about the floodlights. Third version. The first two were taken down by people who disagreed with it.")

<!--more-->

I should explain, for anyone who hasn't had the pleasure, that this isn't the club being mean. It's a planning condition. When the lights went up, some years before my time on the committee, the council agreed to them on the understanding that they'd be off by 21:30, so that the houses behind court three could watch the television in the dark, which is how they prefer it. The condition is in a letter. The letter is in a folder. I've read it more times than I've read anything I actually enjoyed, and it hasn't changed, and I've stopped hoping it will.

So every Tuesday and Thursday from October to March, at 21:29, something happens on all four courts at once. People speed up. You can see it. A doubles four who have been strolling about for an hour, picking balls up one at a time and discussing somebody's knee, suddenly start serving the moment the receiver looks roughly ready. Rallies get short and bad-tempered. Somebody always says "next point wins", and somebody always says that isn't fair, and they're both still saying it when the lights go.

They don't fade, either. I asked once whether they could fade. They go off like someone has died. One moment you're at deuce, the next you're four people standing in a field holding rackets, and the only light is from the clubhouse, where the bar is also closing, also at half nine, because the same letter says so.

I have heard every argument. That two more minutes wouldn't hurt anybody. That the neighbours are asleep by then anyway, which they are not, because they ring. That the lights at the club over the river stay on till ten, which may well be true, and they're welcome to go and play there. One member offered to pay for the extra electricity himself. It was never the electricity. I explained this. He offered more.

The obvious answer is to start earlier, and we've tried. The session is meant to start at seven. It starts at quarter past, after everyone has arrived, warmed up, discussed the draw, objected to the draw, and found a ball that isn't flat. That's fifteen minutes of good light gone before anyone has played a point, and every week we're surprised to lose them again at the other end.

I've raised it at three AGMs. It's recorded each time under Floodlights, and each time the minutes say the same thing: noted.

So that's where we are. The lights go off at half nine. They went off at half nine last winter and they'll go off at half nine next winter, and somewhere on court two there'll be a man at 40-30, ball in hand, about to serve for the set, looking up at the sky as though it has let him down personally.

It has. But it's in the letter.
