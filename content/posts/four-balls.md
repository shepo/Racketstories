The first set took forty-five minutes.

Not because it was close. It finished 6-2. Not because of long rallies either. I've had longer rallies with the ball machine. It took forty-five minutes because of the balls.

![Player waiting to receive](clay-court "This is me. Waiting. Not for the serve. For the counting.")

<!--more-->

Club league, Wednesday night. Nice enough man. Firm handshake. Sensible shoes. Then he goes to serve.

He stops. He looks at the back fence. Two balls there, placed neatly side by side against the netting, like eggs. He pats his left pocket. Two balls. He looks at the fence again. Counts them. Pats the pocket. Then he looks at me.

"That's all four?"

Yes. That's all four. You've just counted them.

Then he serves. Then the point finishes and the ball has to go somewhere, and it can't just go anywhere, it has to go back to the fence, next to the other one, lined up. Then the counting again. Fence, pocket, me.

"All four?"

Every serve. EVERY serve. Not every game. Every point.

And then, about the fourth game, he asks what balls we're playing with.

Dunlop. The tin's on the bench. My tin. It's got Dunlop on it. In big letters.

Fifth game, he asks again. "Sorry, what are these?" Dunlop. Seventh game. "These are the Dunlops, yeah?" They are the Dunlops. They are still the Dunlops. Nobody's switched them while you were at the fence.

I don't know what he thinks happens to tennis balls between points. I don't know what he thinks I'm going to do with one. Pocket it? Swap it for a Slazenger when he's not looking? I've been coaching twenty-two years. I've got a basket of four hundred balls in the car. I do not need his.

In my day you had three balls, and one of them went in the hedge by the second game, and you played the rest of the match with two and nobody counted anything.

Second set was quicker. Thirty-five minutes. Mostly because it got dark and he couldn't see the fence.

I won, by the way. Nobody's asked. 6-2, 6-3. He shook my hand and said "great match" and then he counted the balls back into my tin. Out loud. One, two, three, four.

They were MY balls.
