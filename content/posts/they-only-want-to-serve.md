I timed it. I actually timed it this week, on my phone, because I wanted to KNOW. Here's an hour of my Thursday junior group, ages 11 to 14, eight of them, one court and a basket.

![Rackets on the clubhouse wall](racket-wall "Every one of these rackets has served about four thousand times and rallied about twice.")

<!--more-->

5.00pm. Everyone arrives. Lovely. LOVE them. First question, before coats are off: "Can we do serves?"

5.02. I say warm-up first. Mini tennis, service boxes, gentle. Gorgeous. Two of them are actually rallying!! Ten, eleven, twelve shots!!

5.06. Someone says "can we do serves now" in the voice of a person who's been very patient for four minutes.

5.07. Footwork. Ladders on the ground. They do the ladders like I've asked them to defuse a bomb.

5.15. Forehands from the basket. I feed, they hit, they go to the back. One boy hits every single forehand as hard as he possibly can into the fence and then looks at me like that was the plan. Honestly, the swing is beautiful. The ball is in another postcode.

5.24. "Are we doing serves today though?"

5.25. Fine. FINE. Ten minutes of serves, then we play points. Everyone agrees. Everyone is SO happy. This is the happiest I've seen them since a tournament was cancelled for rain.

5.25 to 5.35. Serves. Eight children, every ball in the basket, the full-ceremony bounce bounce bounce, the toss, the giant swing. About one in six goes in. Nobody minds. One girl has a kick serve already, at twelve, and I nearly cried. I'm not exaggerating. Proper kick. I wanted to ring her parents.

5.35. Right, points! Serve and play it out!

5.35 and twenty seconds. They serve. If it goes in, the returner hits it into the net, or out, or at the server, and everybody is DELIGHTED, because now it's their turn to serve again. I have accidentally invented a game where the rally is the boring bit you get through to reach the next serve.

5.48. I count. Thirteen minutes of points. Longest rally: four. FOUR. That includes the serve.

5.50. "Can we finish with serves?"

5.51. We finish with serves.

So that's the hour. Twenty-plus minutes of serving if you count the bits they sneaked in while I was picking up balls, four minutes of actual rallying, and eight kids walking out saying "that was so good". And it WAS good, that's the annoying part. They love it. The serve is the only shot in tennis where nobody else gets a say, and when you're twelve that's the best feeling in the world.

But a match is mostly the second shot. And the third. And the one after that. Every club I've ever coached at, the juniors who can't rally lose to the juniors who can, every single time, however gorgeous their serve is.

Next week I'm doing a thing where you only earn a serve by winning a rally of six. I'll let you know if there's a riot. There'll be a riot.

P.S. The kick serve girl. If you're a county coach reading this. Just saying.
