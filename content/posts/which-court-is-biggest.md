Our tennis social had finished. It normally does at about nine o'clock. Some people disappear off into the night, and others hang around in the bar for one drink before heading home themselves.

On this occasion the padel group and the squash group finished at about the same time. A social, or it might even have been a competitive match. And somebody in the bar was overheard asking why you can't play padel on a squash court.

The squash players were adamant. It's not big enough. A squash court is smaller than a padel court. And a padel court, they added, is smaller than a tennis court.

![Floodlit courts from above](night-courts "Four tennis courts. Or, depending on who you ask in the bar, eight padel courts and a patio.")

<!--more-->

Well. The padel players were keen to point out that if a padel court is so much smaller, how come we can fit two of them on a tennis court when it's refurbished?

Jon was upset, because that means losing part of the patio. Two padel courts take more room than one tennis court, he said, and in the summer they'd be right up against the fence.

Then the squash coach piped up that you could get four pickleball courts on one tennis court. This really upset the tennis captain, because everybody seems to be coming for another tennis court at the club.

So, after some short, sharp words about which sports are taking over which bits of the club, we were still none the wiser about how big any of the courts actually are. We were, however, very clear on everyone's favourite.

One of the younger members, as they do, asked why we didn't just look it up online. We all said we're trying not to, so that we remember things and use our brains. But it was true. None of us really knew. So the phones came out. Some opened up the chatbot, some Googled, and that's how we came across [RacketWiki](https://racketwiki.org/courts/).

Every racket and bat sport. The rules, the court sizes, the brands. Exactly what we needed, and the whole argument was settled in a few clicks. Which was a shame, really, because I was quite enjoying the banter, even if some of it got a little personal. It's fair to say most people at a racket sports club are pretty competitive. That's why they joined a racket sports club.

For the record:

- Tennis, 23.77 m by 10.97 m. The biggest of the lot, as the tennis captain will be pleased to hear.
- Padel, 20 m by 10 m. Smaller than tennis, as the squash players said.
- Pickleball, 13.41 m by 6.1 m.
- Badminton, 13.4 m by 6.1 m. Pickleball and badminton courts are the same to within a centimetre. Nobody asked. I enjoyed it anyway.
- Squash, 9.75 m by 6.4 m. The smallest. So no, you can't play padel on a squash court. The squash players were right, and they'd like that minuted.

Whether two padel courts or four pickleball courts fit on a tennis court depends on how much room there is around it, and how much of the patio Jon is prepared to give up. The website doesn't cover patios.

While we were on there, somebody looked up [how each sport serves](https://racketwiki.org/rules/). In padel you bounce the ball and hit it at or below waist height. That started a new argument about whose waist.

Same time next week, then.
