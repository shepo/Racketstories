It started, as these things tend to, with a padel player.

The social had finished. Tennis on four courts, padel on two, pickleball in the sports hall, and everybody in the bar by half nine. Someone from the padel end said, quite casually, that a padel court is "basically a tennis court with walls".

Forty minutes later I had a beer mat with three sets of figures on it, none of which agreed with each other.

![Floodlit courts from above](night-courts "Four tennis courts. Or, according to one person in the bar, roughly five padel courts. It is not five.")

<!--more-->

The positions, as they stood at about ten o'clock.

The padel players held that padel and tennis are much the same size. The tennis players held that tennis is far bigger and always has been. A pickleball player said his court was the size of a badminton court, and was told by a badminton player, who I hadn't known we had, that it certainly was not.

Then the serve. A tennis player said a padel serve is just a tennis serve done underarm. A padel player said it has to be below the waist. Someone asked whose waist, the server's or the ball's. Nobody knew. Someone else said a squash serve has to hit the side wall. The one squash player present said it does not. He said it with some feeling.

Nobody in the room was wrong about everything. Most people were wrong about something.

I went home and looked it up, because I'm the chair and that is apparently what the chair is for. There's a site called RacketWiki that draws [every racket sport's court on the same scale](https://racketwiki.org/courts/), side by side, which is either very useful or the end of a perfectly good argument, depending on your view.

For the record:

- Tennis, 23.77 m by 10.97 m. The biggest of the ones we play, as the tennis players will now be telling everyone.
- Padel, 20 m by 10 m. Not the same as tennis. Closer than I'd have guessed.
- Pickleball, 13.41 m by 6.1 m.
- Badminton, 13.4 m by 6.1 m. So the pickleball player was right, to within a centimetre, and the badminton player owes him a drink.
- Squash, 9.75 m by 6.4 m. The smallest of the lot, unless you count table tennis, and I don't.

The serve is on there too, [sport by sport](https://racketwiki.org/rules/). In padel you bounce the ball and hit it at or below waist height. It does not say whose waist, which I expect we'll come back to. In squash the serve goes straight to the front wall and lands in the back quarter on the receiver's side. No side wall required. The squash player has asked me to print that in bold. I haven't.

I've circulated the figures. There have been no replies.

Somebody has already started a new argument about whether the padel walls count as part of the court. I've left the beer mat behind the bar in case it's needed.
