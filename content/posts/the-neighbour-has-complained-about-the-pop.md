We've had a complaint about the pickleball noise. A proper one. Handwritten, two sides, dropped through the clubhouse door on Monday, and the word POP appears in it nine times. I counted!! Nine!!

And look. She's not wrong. Pickleball is loud. It's a hard plastic ball with holes in it hitting a hard flat paddle, and it makes a sharp little POP every single time, and on a Saturday morning with four courts going that's a LOT of pops.

![Players at the net](just-friendly "Every one of these people is making the noise. Every one of them is also having the best morning of their week.")

<!--more-->

The bit that got me was "it is like living next to a shooting range". It isn't!! I've been to a shooting range, on a hen do, and it is NOTHING like it. But I did go and stand at the bottom of her garden fence on Saturday with a free decibel app on my phone, because I wanted to know what she actually hears. Tennis on court one, the normal soft thock, barely moved the number. Pickleball on court five, the closest court to her house, and it jumped every time. POP. POP. POPPOP. Then everyone laughs, because someone's dinked it into the net again, and that's loud too, if I'm honest.

So yes. It's loud. It's not the volume really, it's that it's sharp and it never stops. Tennis has gaps. Pickleball doesn't really do gaps.

But here's what's on court five on a Saturday at ten. Forty-odd people. Half of them had never held a paddle before this year. There's a man who had a knee replaced in March and can't play tennis any more and plays pickleball three times a week now. There are two sisters who hadn't spoken properly in about ten years and now they partner each other and argue about the kitchen line like it's 1987. That's the POP. That's what it sounds like!!

Anyway I went round. With biscuits, obviously. Good ones, not the club ones. And we had a cup of tea and it turned out she doesn't mind the pickleball, she minds not knowing WHEN it's going to be. Some Saturdays it started at nine, some at half eight because people turned up early, and one Sunday a group just carried on till two. She couldn't plan her garden. That's fair!! I'd hate that!!

Here's what we've done, and it's not complicated:

We moved the drop-in to courts two and three, the ones furthest from the houses. Court five is tennis now. Same number of courts, just turned round.

Fixed times. Ten till twelve, Saturday, and that's it. No early starts, no "just one more game" at ten past.

We bought a bag of the quieter balls for the beginners and I'm trying the quieter paddles. They're not silent. Nothing's silent. But it's a softer POP, more of a pup.

And I gave her my number, which Terry says is madness, but she's texted me once in three weeks and it was to say her grandson wants to try it.

It's still loud. It's always going to be a bit loud. But it's forty people having a lovely time between ten and twelve, and now she knows exactly when the lovely time is.

P.S. She came and watched last Saturday. Brought her own chair. I'm counting that as a WIN.
