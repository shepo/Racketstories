Every few months someone sends the committee a proposal to turn a tennis court into two padel courts. It is usually court three, because court three is nearest the car park and furthest from anyone who might object.

The answer is no. I thought I'd explain why, since I seem to be the one who has to.

![Padel doubles](just-friendly "Padel at 7.30pm. Full, loud and having a lovely time. At 10.30am this court was empty.")

<!--more-->

The argument for padel goes like this. The padel courts are always full. The tennis courts are sometimes empty. So swap one. It sounds very sensible if you only come to the club after work, which, to be fair, is when most people who write proposals come to the club.

So last Tuesday I came in at nine and stayed until the lights went off. I'd recommend it to any chair who wants to understand their own club, and to no one else.

At nine there were two coaching groups on the tennis courts and a mixed doubles that has been running since before I was treasurer. The padel courts were empty. At eleven, the retired men's four, the ladies' doubles and a woman hitting serves on her own with a basket. Padel courts, empty. At two, more coaching, a junior who'd been let out of school for a dentist and quite clearly hadn't gone, and a husband and wife who play a single set every Tuesday and never speak during it. Padel, one booking. Two men in work shirts. It looked like a meeting.

At four the juniors arrived and all six tennis courts filled up.

At six the padel courts filled up too, and stayed full until ten. They were very full. They were also very loud. If you have not stood next to a padel court in the evening, it sounds like someone being repeatedly locked out of a conservatory. The houses behind court six have noticed. One of them has written to us. Twice.

So the padel courts are full for four hours a day. The tennis courts are busy from nine in the morning until ten at night. That is not a tennis problem. It's an evening problem, and you don't fix an evening problem by taking a court away from the people who play at eleven on a Tuesday.

I like padel. I've played it twice and I was terrible both times, which is the correct amount. It has brought people into the club who would never have joined for tennis, and they pay their subs and they buy things at the bar. Nobody is asking them to leave.

But tennis is the club. It is the coaching programme, the juniors, the Tuesday mornings, the doubles that's older than the clubhouse. You can add padel to a tennis club. You can't add tennis back once you've dug it up.

So the answer is the same as last time. Both. Side by side. Tennis in the day and the evening, padel in the evening, a bit of give and take on the booking sheet, and somebody, probably me, having a quiet word about the noise after nine.

Nobody is digging up court three.

I have put that in writing, in the minutes, in capitals, which is the first time I've used capitals in the minutes since the biscuit incident.
