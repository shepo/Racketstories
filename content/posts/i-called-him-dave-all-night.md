I need to tell someone and it can't be Barry.

Last Friday I went to a social at a club up the road. As a PLAYER. Not coaching, not running anything, not counting paddles. Just turning up with my racket like a normal person!! I was so excited.

![A crowd of players around an organiser](organiser-cartoon "The draw board at 7.02pm. Somewhere in this crowd is Barry. I didn't know that yet.")

<!--more-->

And between games I got chatting to this lovely man by the drinks table. We had SO much in common. He's got a bad shoulder, I've got a bad shoulder. His daughter's just started pickleball. He thinks the second serve is the hardest shot in tennis, which it IS. We talked about his allotment for a solid ten minutes and I don't even like allotments.

I called him Dave.

Not once. All night. "Good shot, Dave!!" "Dave, are you on court three?" "Lovely to meet you, Dave, see you next week!!" Big wave. Massive wave.

At the end, putting my coat on, a woman behind me says "night Barry". And Dave says "night Sue". And Dave turns round, and sees my face, and I see his face, and there is a moment. A long moment. Longer than the allotment.

He never once corrected me. Not ONCE. And honestly I get it. Once you've let it go twice it's too late. By the fourth "Dave" he'd basically accepted it. He was being Dave for me. That's a kind man. That's a man who deserves to be called Barry.

Here's the thing though, and I've been thinking about it all week. Every social I've ever been to, you meet about twelve new people in two hours. You rotate, you shake hands at the net, someone says their name while the person next door is shouting "OUT!!" and it's gone. Then it's too awkward to ask again, so you spend the evening saying "hiya!" and "you!" and pointing.

So I'm making a rule for my Saturday group. When you rotate, you say your name and the other person says it back. Out loud. Like a weird little ceremony. Terry will hate it, which is a bonus.

And next Friday I'm going back. I've practised in the car. BARRY. Barry, Barry, Barry.

I'll probably call him Gary.

P.S. If you are Barry and you're reading this, I'm so sorry, and I hope the shoulder's better, and how are the leeks?
