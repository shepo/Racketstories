Dear parent on the fence,

Yes, you!! The one with the coffee and the big coat, holding the wire with both hands like it might run off. I see you every Saturday at 9. I'd like you to come onto the court. Not to coach. To PLAY.

![Player waiting to receive](clay-court "This is the face of a ten-year-old who has been told to bend her knees eleven times since breakfast.")

<!--more-->

I know it's not just you. Every club has one of you, sometimes four, and I've coached at enough of them to know you mean well. You love your kid. You've watched a lot of tennis on telly. And from behind the fence it all looks SO simple. Bend your knees. Watch the ball. Racket back early. Move your feet!! FEET!!

Here's the thing though. I'm also saying "watch the ball". At the same time. From a different direction. And your daughter is ten and has one brain and two of us shouting at it, and the ball has gone past her while she worked out which of us to look at. Then she looks at you. Then she looks at me. Then she looks at her shoes. And that's the forehand gone for the morning.

Last month a lad asked me, very quietly, between drills, if his dad could "go and get the car" during the next game. HE'S EIGHT. He's eight and he's working out how to get his dad off the fence. He's got a brilliant future in diplomacy, honestly, but I'd rather he had one in tennis.

So here's my idea, and I've been dying to tell you!!

From next term there's a parents' session on the courts next door, same time as the juniors. Tennis on two courts, pickleball on the other one, because pickleball is the friendliest thing ever invented for grown-ups who haven't held a racket since school. You'll be within shouting distance of your kid and you won't be able to shout at them because you'll be BUSY. Out of breath. Missing easy volleys and laughing about it.

And then something lovely happens. I've seen it at another club and it nearly made me cry by the drinks machine. The kids come off at 10, they wander over to the fence, and THEY watch YOU. And they shout "bend your knees, Dad!!" And Dad bends his knees, and finds out it's actually quite hard, and the next Saturday he holds his coffee a bit more quietly.

You don't have to be any good. Nobody on the parents' court is any good. That's the WHOLE POINT. I'll lend you a racket, I've got a basket of them. Wear trainers. Bring the coffee if you must, there's a bench.

I'm not cross. I promise I'm not cross. I just think you'd have a much better Saturday on this side of the wire, and so would your kid, and so would I.

Come on to the court!!

Love,
Mel

P.S. If you'd really rather watch, that's fine too. There's a lovely spot in the clubhouse window. You can see everything from there and nobody on court can hear a word you say.
