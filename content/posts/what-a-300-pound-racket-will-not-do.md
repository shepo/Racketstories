Third one this month. Walked onto court on Monday, a man I've been coaching since March, and he's holding a racket that cost more than my first car. Took it out of a cover like it was a violin. Said he'd "done a lot of research".

He has not done a lot of research. He has watched a lot of videos. Different thing.

![Level up](level-up "What he thinks happens when you buy the racket. It does not happen.")

<!--more-->

So, for him and the other two, and for whoever's reading this with a basket open on a website, here's what a £300 racket will not do for you.

1. It will not move your feet. Your feet were the problem in March. They are the problem now. The racket has no opinion on your feet.
2. It will not stop you hitting off the back foot. You hit off the back foot with the old one. You will hit off the back foot with this one, but in a nicer colour.
3. It will not turn up to the lesson. Missed three in October. The racket didn't come either.
4. It will not get you a game. Most people I coach play once a week, with me, and then wonder why they don't improve. Hit balls with other people. Lots of them. Any racket will do.
5. It will not make your second serve go in. Nothing makes your second serve go in. I've been coaching twenty-two years and I'm still waiting.
6. It will not stop your partner glaring at you when you poach and miss.
7. It will not string itself. And when it needs restringing, which it will, because you've been told by a man on a video to string it at a tension a professional uses, that's another thirty quid.

That's it. That's the list. Seven things. Could have been seventeen.

In my day you had one racket. Wood. Your dad's, usually, with tape on the handle where he'd broken it. You played with it until it warped, and then you played with it warped, and you got better because you played. Not because of the racket. Because you played.

I'm not saying don't buy one. It's your money. I'm saying the thing that makes you better costs a lot less than £300 and you can get it every week. It's called playing more tennis. Matches. Against people who'll beat you. Then again against people you might beat.

Anyway. He hit with it for an hour on Monday.

Moved his feet a bit better, actually. I think he was scared of scratching it on the court. Didn't want to stretch for a wide one, so he ran for it instead.

So that's one thing it did do. I'll give it that. One.

Still not worth £300.
